01

The shape of the module

A selector engine generic over a trait view of the tree would be a second representation of the tree, and two representations drift. So the engines live beside the tree, and the parsers sit above it.

02

HTML: the spec's algorithm, written down

The tokenizer folds states that share a shape — there are no separate states for every attribute-value flavour — but the tree construction is the specification's list of insertion modes, in the specification's order, with the specification's repairs.

Why it is not "lenient XML"

A lenient XML parser produces one repaired tree. WHATWG produces the tree: a specific one, which is why two browsers agree about <table><div>x<div> and a lenient parser does not.

What "repair" means concretely

Implied <html>, <head>, <body> and <tbody>; cells that close themselves; stray content fostered in front of a table; formatting reopened after a block closed it; misnested markup rearranged by the adoption agency.

Judged by the corpus. html5lib's tree-construction tests are part of the test suite — every file, every case, documents and fragments alike, and every one of them reproduces. scripts/gen_html5lib.py writes the fixture: the inputs, the contexts, and the trees they are expected to produce.
03

The chain: one shape, narrowing

A Selection is the nodes and the document they came from. Every query takes a selection and answers a selection, so the type never changes and the chain never stops.

Identity, not equality

Nodes are compared with physical_equal, never by value: two <li>coffee</li> elements are different nodes, and a set that contains both must keep both.

XPath's two answers

XPath can answer a number. A chain cannot: it is always a set of nodes. So xpath() answers the node-set, and xpath_value() answers the typed XPathValue — nodes, string, number or boolean — for the queries that are questions rather than selections.

04

Writing back

XML: exact

Attribute order, CDATA as CDATA, comments, processing instructions with their raw data, the doctype including an internal subset, <a/> versus <a></a>. Names are Clark-spelled in the tree and prefixed on the way out, using the prefix the document declared; a namespace nothing declared gets ns1 and a declaration on the element that needed it.

HTML: by the rules

Void elements take no close tag, raw-text elements are written raw, & becomes &amp; and a no-break space becomes &nbsp; — WHATWG's own serialization, so what you write is what a browser would read back as the same tree.
